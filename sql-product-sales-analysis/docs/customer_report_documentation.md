# Customer Report View — `gold.report_customer`

## 📋 Overview

The `gold.report_customer` view provides a consolidated, customer-centric overview of sales performance, purchasing behavior, customer demographics, and customer segmentation.

It transforms transactional sales data into customer-level metrics, enabling businesses to analyze customer value, purchasing frequency, recency, and long-term engagement.

**Key use cases:**
- Customer segmentation and loyalty analysis.
- Sales performance and revenue analysis.
- Identification of high-value customers.
- Customer purchasing behavior analysis.
- Business intelligence dashboards and reporting.

## 🏗️ Architecture & Transformation Logic

The view uses a Common Table Expression (CTE) pipeline to organize data extraction, aggregation, and analytical calculations into three logical stages.

### 1. `base_query` — Data Preparation

Retrieves transactional data from `gold.fact_sales` and enriches it with customer information from `gold.dim_customers`.

**Operations performed:**
- Joins sales transactions with customer demographic data using `customer_key`.
- Retrieves order details, product information, sales amounts, and quantities.
- Combines customers' first and last names into a single `customer_name` field.
- Calculates customer age using the birthdate and current date.
- Excludes records with null order dates.

### 2. `customer_aggregation` — Customer-Level Aggregation

Groups the prepared data by customer to calculate purchasing and sales metrics.

**Metrics calculated:**
- **Total Orders:** Number of distinct orders placed.
- **Total Sales:** Total revenue generated by the customer.
- **Total Quantity:** Total number of items purchased.
- **Total Products:** Number of product records associated with the customer's transactions.
- **Last Order:** Most recent purchase date.
- **Customer Lifespan:** Number of calendar-month boundaries between the first and last recorded orders.

### 3. Final SELECT — Customer Analytics

Calculates additional analytical metrics and assigns customer classifications.

- **Recency:** Months since the customer's most recent order.
- **Age Segmentation:** Groups customers into predefined age categories.
- **Customer Segmentation:** Classifies customers as `VIP`, `Regular`, or `New` based on lifespan and total sales.
- **Average Order Value (AOV):** Total sales divided by the number of distinct orders.
- **Average Monthly Spend:** Total sales divided by customer lifespan, with special handling for zero-month lifespans.

## 📊 Column Definitions

| Column | Description |
|---|---|
| `customer_key` | Surrogate key identifying the customer in the data warehouse. |
| `customer_number` | Business identifier assigned to the customer. |
| `customer_name` | Customer's full name, combining first and last names. |
| `age` | Estimated age calculated from the customer's birthdate. |
| `total_order` | Number of distinct orders placed by the customer. |
| `total_sales` | Total sales amount generated by the customer. |
| `totla_quantity` | Total quantity of items purchased. |
| `total_product` | Number of non-null product-key records across the customer's transactions. |
| `last_order` | Date of the customer's most recent recorded order. |
| `recency` | Number of calendar-month boundaries between the last order and the current date. |
| `age_segmentation` | Customer age category: `20-29`, `30-39`, `40-49`, or `above 50`. |
| `customer_segment` | Customer classification based on lifespan and total sales. |
| `avg_order_value` | Average sales amount per distinct order. |
| `avg_monthly_spend` | Sales amount divided by customer lifespan in months, with special handling when lifespan is zero. |

## 🎯 Customer Segmentation Rules

### Age Segmentation

| Condition | Segment |
|---|---|
| Age below 20 | `above 50`* |
| Age between 20 and 29 | `20-29` |
| Age between 30 and 39 | `30-39` |
| Age between 40 and 49 | `40-49` |
| All other ages, including 50 and above | `above 50` |

*The first rule reflects the current SQL implementation and should be corrected because customers under 20 are incorrectly classified as `above 50`.*

### Behavioral Segmentation

| Segment | Business Rule |
|---|---|
| `VIP` | Lifespan ≥ 12 months and total sales ≥ 5,000 |
| `Regular` | Lifespan ≥ 12 months and total sales < 5,000 |
| `New` | All remaining customers |

These rules use customer lifespan and cumulative sales as proxies for loyalty and value. They are business-defined thresholds rather than universal customer segmentation standards.

## 📈 Key Performance Indicators

### Average Order Value (AOV)

Measures the average sales amount generated per distinct order.

**Formula:**

`AOV = Total Sales / Total Orders`

A higher AOV may indicate that customers spend more per transaction.

### Customer Recency

Measures how recently a customer made a purchase.

**Formula:**

`Recency = Current Date − Last Order Date`

The SQL implementation expresses this difference in calendar-month boundaries. Lower recency values generally indicate more recent purchasing activity.

### Customer Lifespan

Measures the elapsed time between a customer's first and last recorded orders.

**Formula:**

`Lifespan = Last Order Date − First Order Date`

The SQL implementation measures this in calendar-month boundaries. A customer whose first and last orders occur in the same month has a lifespan of zero.

### Average Monthly Spend

Estimates sales generated per month of recorded customer lifespan.

**Formula:**

`Average Monthly Spend = Total Sales / Lifespan in Months`

When lifespan is zero, the current SQL returns total sales instead. In this case, the result represents total sales rather than a true monthly average.

## ⚙️ Technical Notes & Limitations

- **Age accuracy:** `DATEDIFF(year, birthdate, GETDATE())` counts year boundaries and can overestimate age before the customer's birthday. A birthday-aware calculation is more accurate.
- **Age segmentation:** Customers younger than 20 are incorrectly assigned to `above 50` under the current CASE expression.
- **Product count:** `COUNT(product_key)` counts non-null product-key records, not necessarily distinct products. Use `COUNT(DISTINCT product_key)` if the intended metric is the number of unique products purchased.
- **Quantity naming:** `totla_quantity` contains a spelling mistake. Consider renaming it to `total_quantity`.
- **Customer lifespan:** `DATEDIFF(month, ...)` counts month boundaries, not complete elapsed months.
- **Average monthly spend:** Customers with zero-month lifespans receive their total sales as the result, which is not directly comparable with the monthly averages of other customers.
- **Null customer attributes:** Because the view uses a `LEFT JOIN`, transactions without a matching customer record may be grouped together when customer attributes are null.
- **Data freshness:** The use of `GETDATE()` means age and recency can change when the view is queried, without requiring a stored-data refresh.

## 💼 Business Value

The `gold.report_customer` view provides a reusable analytical dataset for answering questions such as:

- Which customers generate the most revenue?
- How many customers qualify as VIPs?
- How recently have customers purchased?
- Which age groups contribute most to sales?
- What is the average order value across customer segments?
- Which customers may need re-engagement campaigns?

By consolidating customer-level metrics into a single view, this dataset simplifies reporting and supports data-driven marketing, sales, and customer-retention decisions.
